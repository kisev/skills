# Plain Explanation Workflow

## Purpose

Explain one complex concept so a reader without the field's background can
follow the mechanism and its consequences. The explanation serves the reader's
actual knowledge, not a fixed age or a fixed template.

## Method

1. Establish what is knowable about the reader from the request, the
   conversation, and supplied context; calibrate depth and vocabulary to that.
   Without evidence about the reader, assume an intelligent adult outside the
   field, never a child.
2. Identify the core of the mechanism: what it does, why it exists, and what
   changes for the reader. Build the explanation around that core.
3. Use plain words and concrete everyday anchors. Introduce each unavoidable
   term with a one-line gloss at first use, then keep that term consistent.
4. Preserve every essential constraint, exception, and tradeoff in exact form.
   Simplify the wording, never the conditions.
5. Preserve uncertainty as uncertainty: keep "unknown", "not measured", or
   "disputed" instead of a rounded or invented value.
6. Use at most one analogy, and only when it genuinely maps the mechanism;
   state where the analogy breaks down. An analogy illustrates; it never
   replaces exact conditions, numbers, or boundaries.
7. Do not re-explain what the reader already knows and do not pad the answer to
   look thorough. Compare the result with the source: nothing added, nothing
   inverted, no fact or number introduced.

## Boundary

- Apply `references/clarity-rules.md` for consistent terms and the separation
  of facts, decisions, assumptions, and recommendations; the result keeps the
  source's obligations, confidence, numbers, exceptions, and boundaries.
- When the supplied material lacks a fact the explanation seems to need, keep
  the gap explicitly unknown or ask one bounded question; never fill it with a
  plausible guess.
- Apply `humanize` when the explanation is substantial user-facing prose; a
  short chat answer does not require loading another skill.
