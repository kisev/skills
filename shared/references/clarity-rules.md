# Clarity Rules

Compact shared rules for clear task language. Apply them to questions, goals,
tasks, work items, and rewritten text. They complement `humanize`, which owns
natural prose; they do not create a mandatory sequence of editing passes. Apply
them once as a checklist, and do not rewrite text that is already clear.

- Clarity is always mandatory; a detailed plain-language explanation is added
  only when the reader needs it. Do not re-explain terms the audience already
  knows.
- Use one consistent term for one concept; do not alternate synonyms for the
  same thing.
- State the actor, the action, the conditions, and the scope wherever the
  meaning depends on them.
- Mark what is a fact, an agreed decision, an assumption, or a recommendation.
  Agreed requirements stay separated from new proposals; never present a
  recommendation as an agreement.
- Before finishing, verify that every binding condition in the result — each
  acceptance criterion, constraint, and stop condition — follows from an
  agreed requirement or an applicable rule. Anything added while formulating
  checks or transferring the statement stays a visibly separated proposal or
  assumption; it never becomes an obligation or is carried forward as a user
  decision.
- Simplification never changes the obligation, the degree of confidence,
  numbers, exceptions, or boundaries. When meaning is missing, ask one bounded
  question or keep the gap explicitly unknown; never invent it.
- Examples and analogies illustrate; they never replace exact conditions,
  constraints, or numbers.
